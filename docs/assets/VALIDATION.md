`validate_bottle_thread.py` requires ±20 µm final pitch residual, at least 50 µm signed axial travel under ±0.04 N·m, less than 0.1 mm maximum transient constraint error, and at most 0.06 rad no/low torque drift in the short cases. The long case allows 0.3 mm final and 0.4 mm transient error because the free bottle rests on the ground and the axial drive is compliant. These are **engineering acceptance thresholds for this sample**, not measured cap specifications. The 600-step run verifies near-three-turn motion and a finite end stop when detachment is disabled. Optional single-direction detachment passed a two-environment fixture; automatic re-engagement is unsupported. Every run checked finite root pose/velocity; no NaN or runaway velocity occurred in these fixtures. The initial contact probe moves the bottle assembly **once before stepping**; no root pose writes are made during the simulation loop.

## Follow-up before RL task design

1. Identify the exact hardware revision and wrist mounting transform from serial number/BOM; measure real finger drive effort and pose limits. The official USD currently sets every finger drive `maxForce` to 2 N·m, which differs from several MJCF limits.
2. Replace the example bottle with measured CAD, thread profile, mass and inertia; measure actual static breakaway and running torque. Test threaded contact under grasp and across longer horizons. The unforced drift should be reduced before dense contact RL.
3. Add physically justified cap re-engagement; the current release transition is one-way and requires the cap to be reinitialized for another trial.
4. Validate strict wrist speed enforcement under collision, multi-environment throughput and checkpointed train/play on the target workstation.
